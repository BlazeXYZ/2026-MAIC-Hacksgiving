# Hubs notes

Taken from the ComForce MKE prototype (the Hubs shelf and the Quick Exit lesson) and the weekend plan. This is the piece the hubs work is built from.

## What a hub is

Every ComForce program has a hub with the same basics, plus tools of its own. The same four tabs show up in the same place in every hub, so moving between them feels familiar:

1. Threads
2. Map (the label can change: Pantry map, Search map, Shelter map)
3. Requests
4. News

A divider comes after those four. Anything past it belongs to that hub only, and it is marked "This hub." Food has the stocking advisor. Missing Persons has the search grid. Domestic Safety has Find a way out.

A pinned post welcomes new members and says how that hub works.

Hubs in the prototype: Missing Persons Network, Food & Mutual Aid, Domestic Safety (private), Rides & Transportation, Youth Advocacy · Cycle Breakers, Disaster Relief, MKE Parents Club, Activist & Advocacy Podcast.

Domestic Safety says "Private" on the card. It never shows who joined, and the member count is not a number.

Posts with a home address, a phone number, or an email are held. The author is told why in plain words. The post does not go public until that detail is gone.

When a thread asks a question the hub already knows how to answer, the answer shows up in the thread, marked as automatic, in the italic voice so it is not mistaken for a neighbor.

ComForce staff set up a new hub with a community partner. Neighbors can suggest one. They do not publish it themselves.

## Quick Exit, from the lesson

Exit is on every screen, top right. On a computer, Esc does the same thing.

The screen becomes a bus schedule immediately. That is the disguise, not a settings page.

Coming back is deliberate: three taps on the blue bar. The lesson is shown once, up front, because burying it in settings is too late.

Shake-to-exit stays available for a phone, including when someone's hands are full. It is a setting, not the default.

## What this build adds

Threads work like a small forum. A neighbor posts a title and a note, other people reply, and anyone can mark a post helpful. Sort is Hot, New, or Top. Pinned welcomes stay at the top.

Trust scores are for people and employees who keep helping. Staff type the number and a short note. The app does not calculate it, rank posts to produce it, or change it when someone is marked helpful. A badge on the name shows the number staff entered. Clearing the score removes it.

Jordan Hale is the signed-in ComForce employee in the demo, so the trust control is available on names and under the initial menu.
